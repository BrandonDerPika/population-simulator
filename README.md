# Population Simulator

[**Play the Simulation**](https://brandonderpika.github.io/population-simulator/)

A small interactive population pyramid simulator for experimenting with how demographic conditions shape a population over time.

The simulator represents the population in five-year age groups and lets you adjust demographic settings, advance through time, and compare the population at different points in the simulation.

## Features

### Population Settings

Customize the conditions that influence population growth and age distribution

- **Birth Rate:** Set the rate of births in the population.
- **Standard of Living:** Adjust the standard of living used by the simulation.
- **Mortality Curve:** Change the historical mortality curve used by the simulation
- **Male War Losses:** Model additional population losses among males.
- **Scale:** Adjust the scale used to display the population pyramid.

### Disease Simulation

Configure an outbreak and explore how it affects different parts of the population

- **Affected Groups:** Choose which parts of the population are affected.
- **Mortality Rate:** Set the disease's mortality rate.
- **Exposure Rate:** Set how readily the disease spreads through the population.
- **Vaccination Coverage:** Adjust the share of the population covered by vaccination.

### Population Pyramid and History

The main visualization displays the population as horizontal bars grouped by age, with five years between each age group.

Advance the simulation in five-year increments, then navigate back through previous time points to compare how the population has changed.

A graph at the bottom provides another view of the population's development over time.

## Purpose

This project started as a small experiment in population demographics. It is interesting to see how changes made today can affect the age distribution of a population decades later, especially when factors such as birth rates, mortality, war losses, and disease are involved.

The simulator is an exploratory model rather than a formal demographic forecasting tool. Its results depend on the assumptions and simplified rules implemented in the simulation.
